# Retention

## Overview

A `Goal Retention` metric measures how many users come back to complete a goal at or after a defined period of time has passed since a reference point.
It tells you whether users continue performing the same action after a delay, rather than only measuring their initial behavior.

The reference point is set with the `Retention Time Reference` and can be either:

- **First exposure**: the moment the user is first exposed to the experiment. The user does not need to have completed the goal before.
A user is retained if they complete the goal at or after `first exposure + retention period`.
- **First achievement**: the moment the user first completes the goal. Only users who have completed the goal at least once can be retained.
A user is retained if they complete the goal again at or after `first achievement + retention period`.

The retention period is a minimum delay, not a window: any matching goal event from that point onwards counts, up to the end of the experiment's analysis period.

This makes it possible to measure longer-term engagement and whether your experiment influences users’ likelihood to come back and repeat an action.

## Examples

```javascript
context.track("purchase", {
  price: 1000,
  order_number: "0000982532",
  product_id: "ABC123",
  category_id: "ZYZ123"
});
```

Imagine you want to measure how many users **purchase again 7 or more days after their first purchase**.

You can create a `Purchase Retention (7 days)` metric by:
- Selecting the `purchase` goal
- Setting the `Retention Period` to 7 days
- Setting the `Retention Time Reference` to the user's first achievement (their first purchase)

Then the metric counts users who satisfy:
- they completed a first purchase
- and they completed another purchase at least 7 days after that first purchase

If you set the `Retention Time Reference` to the user's first exposure instead, the metric counts users who
made a purchase at least 7 days after they were first exposed to the experiment. No earlier purchase is needed:
a user whose only purchase happens 10 days after exposure is retained, while a user who only purchased 2 days after exposure is not.

:::caution
If your metric uses [CUPED](../variance-reduction-cuped), the lookback window must be equal or larger than the retention period.
:::

**More examples**

- `Checkout Return (24 hours)`:
Users who completed a purchase 24 hours or more after their first exposure to the experiment.

- `Content Return (3 days)`:
Users who read an article again 3 or more days after their first reading.

- `Subscription Renewal (30 days)`:
Users who performed a renewal action again 30 or more days after their first renewal.

## Good to know

- A user who only returns before the retention period has elapsed is not counted as retained.
- Retention can only be observed while the experiment is running. Users exposed close to the end of the experiment have less time to qualify,
so long retention periods need experiments that run well beyond the retention period.
- Great for measuring long-term impact rather than immediate conversions.
- Helps identify behaviors such as repeat purchases, content revisits, subscription renewals, or delayed engagement.
- The metric counts users, not events — a user either meets retention criteria or does not.
- Filters on the goal event apply to the qualifying event (for example: “retention among users who purchased category ZYZ123”).
- Retention metrics are often more stable and less noisy than total repeated activations, since each user contributes at most once.
- Changing the retention period alters the meaning of the metric and requires a new version.
- Useful for experiments where the impact is delayed: onboarding experiences, notifications, emails, recommendations, reminders, etc.
