# Setting up a Holdout

A step by step guide to creating a holdout, configuring it and starting it.

Open the **Holdouts** page from the left navigation. It lists every holdout on your platform, along
with its status, tracking unit, traffic allocation, metrics and schedule.

<Image maxWidth="48rem" centered img="holdout/holdouts-list.png" alt="The Holdouts page listing holdout groups" />

## Creating a holdout

You need to be a Team Admin of the team the holdout will belong to. To create a general holdout,
you need a global role with holdout permissions, such as `FullAdmin`. See
[Permissions](./overview#permissions).

Click **New holdout**, then choose:

- **Tracking unit** (required): the unit used to identify visitors. It is fixed once the holdout is
  created, and only experiments using this unit can join it.
- **Teams** (optional): the teams the holdout belongs to. It covers the experiments of these teams
  and their child teams. Leave it empty to create a general holdout, which belongs to your top-level
  team and covers experiments from all teams.

A team can only have one holdout per tracking unit, so there can be at most one general holdout per
tracking unit.

Click **Create holdout** to open its configuration page.

<Image maxWidth="40rem" centered img="holdout/configure-holdout.png" alt="The Configure Holdout page" />

## General

Give the holdout a **Name** and an optional **Description**. These appear on the Holdouts page and at
the top of the report, so describe what the holdout covers, for example
`Holdout group for all experiments tracking visitors by device`.

## Configuration

### Holdout type

Choose whether to hold out one group or two. This affects how the holdout results are compared.

- **Simple holdout** (default): one held-out control group, kept out of all experiments and
  compared against everyone else. Best for measuring the total cumulative impact of your
  experimentation programme.
- **Advanced holdout**: adds a second held-out group that only sees features already shipped to
  100% of traffic, so you can isolate the impact of experiments from the impact of what was kept.

:::caution
Advanced holdouts use more of your traffic. Because of the additional held-out group, fewer visitors
remain available for experiments, and experiments may need to run longer to collect enough
participants for reliable results.
:::

See [Simple and advanced holdouts](./overview#simple-and-advanced-holdouts) for how the two types
compare.

### Tracking unit

The **tracking unit** chosen when the holdout was created is shown here and cannot be changed. Only
experiments that track visitors with this unit are part of this holdout.

### Teams

The teams the holdout belongs to. The holdout covers the experiments of these teams and their child
teams, and its teams decide who can view and manage it. Leave it empty for a general holdout.

### Experiment inclusion

Choose how experiments join this holdout.

- **Opt-out** (default): all experiments on the holdout's tracking unit, from its teams and their
  child teams, are included automatically. Specific experiments can be excluded from the experiment
  form.
- **Opt-in**: experimenters add their experiment to this holdout from the experiment form. Only
  experiments from the holdout's teams and their child teams can be added, and only opted-in
  experiments take part.

### Traffic allocation

Choose what share of this unit's traffic is held out, between **1% and 10%**.

| Holdout type | Recommended allocation |
| --- | --- |
| Simple | **3%** |
| Advanced | **6%**, with 3% for each held-out group |

The allocation covers the held-out groups only. The **No Holdout** group they are compared against
is sampled from the rest of the population to match their size.

:::tip
Increasing the allocation lets you detect smaller cumulative effects, but keeps more visitors away
from the improvements you have shipped. If you are unsure, start with the recommended allocation.
:::

## Metrics

Select the metrics used to evaluate the holdout, from your existing metric catalogue. A holdout can
use up to **5 metrics**.

Only metrics compatible with the holdout's tracking unit are shown, and the active filter is
displayed above the selector. A metric defined on a different unit cannot be computed for this
holdout, so it does not appear in the list.

Choose metrics that reflect the overall health of your product rather than the success of any one
feature. A holdout measures the combined effect of everything you shipped, so top-line business
metrics are the ones worth watching.

## Schedule

### Duration

Choose a **duration** of **3 months**, **6 months**, **12 months** or **Until stopped**. Set a
**start date**, and the end date is calculated for you.

A longer period accumulates more experiments and more data, which makes small cumulative effects
easier to detect. A shorter period gives you a completed, archived result sooner and refreshes the
randomisation more often. Three months suits platforms running many experiments; six or twelve
months suit those with fewer experiments or slower-moving metrics.

A holdout set to run **Until stopped** has no end date. It runs as a single period until you stop
it.

### Restart automatically when holdout ends

When this is enabled, a new holdout period with the same configuration begins immediately once the
current one ends, and visitors are randomised again from scratch. A visitor who was in the holdout
group during one period might not be in it during the next.

Automatic restart does not apply to holdouts set to run **Until stopped**, since they have no end
date. It is also disabled when a holdout is stopped manually.

## Starting and stopping

Save the configuration, then start the holdout when you are ready. Running holdouts show a
**Running** status on the Holdouts page, along with their current period's dates.

:::caution
Changes saved while a holdout is running only apply to the **next** period. This covers every
setting, including experiment inclusion. The current period keeps the configuration it started
with, so its dataset stays internally consistent. To change a running holdout immediately, stop it
first, then save.
:::

## Adding and excluding experiments

Experiments join and leave a holdout from the experiment form, not from the holdout itself.

With **Opt-in** inclusion, anyone who can edit an experiment can add it to the holdout.

With **Opt-out** inclusion, every experiment on the holdout's tracking unit, from its teams and
their child teams, is included automatically. The Team Admins of the holdout's team, and users with
the permission to exclude experiments from holdouts, can exclude an individual experiment. Owning
the experiment is not enough on its own.

Exclude an experiment only when withholding a change is genuinely not acceptable, such as a legal or
compliance requirement, or a fix that has to reach every visitor. Each exclusion means the holdout
group has been exposed to that experiment, which moves the baseline a little further from the
unchanged product and weakens the comparison.

If you expect to exclude many experiments, consider **Opt-in** inclusion instead, so that only the
experiments you choose take part.
