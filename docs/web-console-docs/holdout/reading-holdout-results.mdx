# Reading Holdout Results

A holdout report shows the cumulative impact of your experiments on a tracking unit, measured
against a group of visitors who were kept out of them.

Open a holdout from the **Holdouts** page to see its detail view. The **Overview** tab shows the
results of a single period, and the **Iterations** tab lists every period the holdout has run.

Members of a holdout's team can view its results, and everyone can view the general holdout's
results. See [Permissions](./overview#permissions).

## The Overview tab

<Image maxWidth="48rem" centered img="holdout/holdout-results.png" alt="The Overview tab of a holdout showing the metrics table for the current period" />

The top of the page shows the holdout's name, its description, and badges for its holdout type
(**Advanced**), its experiment inclusion setting (**Opt-out** or **Opt-in**) and its teams.

Below that, the period header names the period you are looking at, its status and its scheduled
start and end dates. Use the arrows on the right to move between periods without leaving the tab.

A summary card repeats the holdout's configuration: tracking unit, traffic allocation, duration and
schedule.

### The variants

Every metric is reported for the same groups. Each variant is compared with the control.

| Variant | Name | What it represents | Holdout type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control | **Holdout** | Visitors kept out of all experiments. The baseline. | Simple and advanced |
| Variant 1 | **No Holdout** | A sample of the experiment-exposed population, sized to match the holdout group. | Simple and advanced |
| Variant 2 | **Full on only** | Visitors held out of experiments who only see features already shipped to 100% of traffic. | Advanced only |

All rows carry equal participant counts by design. **No Holdout** is sampled from the full
population rather than being every remaining visitor, so that the comparison is between groups of
the same size. A **Sampling** toggle above the metrics indicates that this equal-size sampling is
in effect.

### Reading a metric

Each metric card lists every variant with its participants, value and mean, followed by a
**Metric total** row. For each variant, the statistics of its comparison with the control are
shown:

- **Confidence**: how confident the test is that the observed difference is real.
- **Relative impact**: how much the variant differs from the holdout baseline.
- **Impact per day**: the effect expressed as a daily rate.

Because Control is the holdout, a **positive relative impact on No Holdout means your experiments
are performing better than the unchanged product**. That is the outcome you are hoping for: the
changes you shipped add up to a measurable gain over doing nothing.

A negative or flat result is equally informative. It suggests the individual wins you recorded did
not accumulate, whether through decay over time, interaction between experiments, or effects that
were smaller than they first appeared.

In an advanced holdout, **Full on only** shows the impact of the features you shipped on their
own, without exposure to the experiments that were tested but not kept. Comparing it with **No
Holdout** tells you how much of the overall impact comes from what you shipped, and how much comes
from running experiments.

:::info
For a fuller explanation of confidence, impact and the statistics behind them, see
[Interpreting metrics in experiment results](/docs/web-console-docs/experiments/interpreting-metrics-in-experiment-results).
:::

### Experiments in this period

Below the metrics, the **Experiments in this period** table lists every experiment that ran at any
point during the period, with its status, start date, and the date it was stopped or put Full on.

Use it to connect a change in the holdout's results with the experiments that were running at the
time.

### Checking results before the period ends

Holdouts are analysed once per day using Fully Sequential testing, so the numbers are valid whenever
you look at them. You can check a running holdout at any point during its period without waiting
for it to finish, and without the risk of an inflated false positive rate that repeatedly checking
a fixed horizon test would carry.

Because results refresh daily rather than continuously, the figures you see reflect the most recent
analysis rather than the last few minutes of traffic.

## The Iterations tab

<Image maxWidth="48rem" centered img="holdout/holdout-iterations.png" alt="The Iterations tab listing completed and running holdout periods" />

Each period the holdout has run appears here, newest first, with its status, date range and total
participants. The current period shows as **Running**; earlier ones show as **Completed**.

Expand any period to see its results.

### Periods are independent

Each period is a separate dataset with its own randomisation and its own results. Periods are never
merged or averaged, because the holdout group is drawn fresh each time and the experiments running
during one period are not the same as those running during the next.

Read the sequence as a trend rather than a single accumulating number. A run of periods each showing
a positive impact is strong evidence that your experimentation programme is delivering; a series
that flattens over time is a signal worth investigating.

Completed periods stay accessible indefinitely, so the archive builds into a long-run record of what
your experimentation has been worth.
